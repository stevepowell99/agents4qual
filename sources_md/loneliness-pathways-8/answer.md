# Pathways to loneliness, and what keeps it going

Speaking of their own lives, these young people most often trace loneliness to being physically alone through circumstance ({t_linkrolewhose.c1}: I08, I10, I27, I43), and to conflict or distance in the family ({t_linkrolewhose.c3}: I22, I27, I43); on the way to it, low self-worth or social anxiety makes them stay away from people ({t_linkwhose.c5}: I10, I27, I41). Fewer state of their own case an abusive or controlling partner ({t_linkwhose.c10}: I10, I27), moving somewhere new ({t_linkwhose.c16}: I27, I43), not fitting in ({t_linkwhose.c17}: I18, I43), having nobody to open up to ({t_linkwhose.c20}: I39, I43), withdrawing ({t_linkwhose.c21}: I27, I41) and family trouble leading to withdrawing ({t_linkwhose.c12}: I22, I27). Losing someone close ({t_linkwhose.c27}: I22) and shallow friendships ({t_linkwhose.c38}: I10), which several raise in general terms, are each stated of the speaker's own life by I22 or I10 alone.

What keeps loneliness going is told mostly about other people. Loneliness leading to low mood is said of others by {t_linkrolewhose.c2} (I08, I18, I41, I43) but of the speaker's own case only by I27 ({t_linkrolewhose.c22}). Withdrawing that deepens existing loneliness is said only of others ({t_linkrolewhose.c18}: I08, I39); those who say withdrawing made them lonely ({t_linkrolewhose.c19}: I27, I41) describe how it started, not how it was kept going. Of their own case, {t_rolewhose.c4} describe anything that keeps their loneliness going (I10, I27, I43), against {t_rolewhose.c3} who describe it of others (I08, I18, I39, I41, I43); with eight people that difference is not clear.

## Pathways into loneliness

Being physically alone through circumstance is the route the most people describe from their own life ({t_linkrolewhose.c1}): working alone, being out of work, a bedroom at night, and being stuck at home with a baby. "I started to feel really lonely, working alone in the studio" [c_links.gwxc6]; "from Monday to Friday, nine to five, there's no one really around" [c_links.g473e]; "I would feel lonely, because it's just literally where I’m physically alone" [c_links.wne5s]; and, for a young mother in a mother and baby unit, "it does get quite lonely at times" [c_links.cnjx8]. I39 and I43 also say it of other people ({t_linkwhose.c11}) [c_links.6aewp, c_links.dt6fs]. Against it, I10 adds of the same period "Obviously, I was never really lonely because I'd always have somebody there with me to talk to" (I10), I27 says of time on her own "I feel like those are my best times really. When I’m without people." (I27), and I39 says "Being alone can be a positive, but can also be a negative" (I39).

Conflict or distance in the family leads to loneliness in the own accounts of {t_linkrolewhose.c3}, and nobody says it only of others: a mother and her boyfriend "always fighting and arguing" [c_links.j5zhf], growing up with no siblings ("And those were definitely like some like lonely times." [c_links.sbts4]), and a family whose members now live separate lives: "But the loneliness is comes and goes here and there, cause you got nephews and nieces and you see them, sometimes just miss them." [c_links.wf5u7]. For I22 and I27 the same family trouble also makes them keep away ({t_linkwhose.c12}): "I just always wanted to be with myself in my room" [c_links.qjyre]; "I like to avoid problems. Even if that means… missing out on stuff and not seeing certain people." [c_links.ywyea]. Against it, I22 says of her family not getting on, "it doesn’t actually bother me anymore" (I22).

Low self-worth and social anxiety lead to withdrawing in the own accounts of {t_linkwhose.c5}: dread before going out ("like do not go out. Just stay inside." [c_links.xwdun]), anxiety among strangers [c_links.7tmpj], and guilt and anxiety that stop someone calling family she has lost touch with ("I just don’t even know how to pick up the phone" [c_links.tsufw]). I41 and I43 also say it of others ({t_linkwhose.c14}), including self-questioning that ends in cancelled plans [c_links.wb753]. I43, who describes that, says of himself "So I never really had self-doubt like that" (I43).

An abusive or controlling partner leads to loneliness in the own accounts of {t_linkwhose.c10}: for I27, a partner whose home area, where she had nobody, she moved to ("I literally felt like that was a way for… his dad- my son’s dad to make me f… feel like I only had him" [c_links.f8h7g]); for I10, services that turned her away while she was in an abusive relationship [c_links.nepvf].

A move or a new start leads to loneliness in the own accounts of {t_linkwhose.c16}: "That first day at work" [c_links.67f7j]; "it was quite lonely because I just stayed at home most of the time being because I didn't have anyone there" [c_links.drydu]; ending up "just me and my son" after moving [c_links.puzbh]. I18 and I39 say it of others ({t_linkwhose.c15}) [c_links.eq2ny]. Against it, I39 says experience has changed this for her: "So I can not feel so lonely in that situation, because I know that I would sort of get to know people." (I39), and I43 says of his new job that "slowly that loneliness disappears" (I43).

Not fitting in, or being shut out, leads to loneliness in the own accounts of {t_linkwhose.c17}: racism at university ("And that’s where I felt very lonely like three years being in a place where it’s predominantly White" [c_links.6jr5h]), being the only one of one's kind ("you can instantly end up very lonely when there’s no one like you in the place you are" [c_links.cnq63]) and outgrowing a peer group whose norms one no longer shares [c_links.m6che]. I18, I39 and I41 say it of others ({t_linkwhose.c6}) [c_links.2trxs, c_links.2ug7u, c_links.x9wcd]. Against it, I18 says of how others see him, "It’s not really a source of that much trouble or problems or negative feelings for me." (I18), and I43 says of the racism, "it didn't really affect me that much" (I43).

Having nobody to open up to leads to loneliness in the own accounts of {t_linkwhose.c20}: holding something in when upset [c_links.pwezc], and having "certain stuff you wanna talk about, you're not comfortable with your friends or your family" [c_links.maehv]. I08, I39 and I41 say it of others ({t_linkwhose.c8}), including the expectation that men hide their emotions: "men keeping their emotions pend up, makes them feel isolated cause they can’t share that with anyone" [c_links.2pg3e]. Against it, I08 says of not being able to disclose, "Personally, I'll just kind of like, I'll just try to get on with it" (I08).

Some routes are stated of the speaker's own life by a single person, though others raise them in general. Losing someone close: I22 of her dog ({t_linkwhose.c27}): "that actually made me feel lonely, ‘cause obviously my dog was a big part of my life" [c_links.ntn6a]; I18, I41 and I43 speak of grief, the feared death of someone close and break-ups in general ({t_linkwhose.c4}) [c_links.gcumv, c_links.cefcj, c_links.k3fkt]. Shallow friendships: I10 of herself ({t_linkwhose.c38}): "they don’t hold any longevity and I think that can allow you to feel lonely" [c_links.xryc7], and she adds "I’m more content with it now than I have been" (I10); I18 and I43 say it of others ({t_linkwhose.c18}). Having no partner: I43 only ({t_linkwhose.c34}) [c_links.27wd9]. Social media is said only of others ({t_linkwhose.c19}) [c_links.hpkwf, c_links.etq3q]; of himself, I08 says "I feel like without social media, I feel like I would be depressed" (I08).

## What keeps loneliness going

All but I22 describe something that keeps loneliness going ({t_roleonly.c2}), but of their own case only {t_rolewhose.c4} do: I10's guilt and anxiety about calling family she has lost touch with [c_links.kawyr, c_links.tsufw], I27's isolation getting "quite depressing" [c_links.seu76], and I43's deep thinking that "can just bring it all up in your mind" [c_links.n9x4c]. The accounts of what keeps loneliness going are otherwise told about other people ({t_rolewhose.c3}: I08, I18, I39, I41, I43).

Loneliness leads to low mood, depression or brooding in what {t_linkrolewhose.c2} say of others: "I feel like when you're lonely it’s easy for you to be depressed" [c_links.h9apq]; "most people’s depression it’s because inside of them they feel like they are alone" [c_links.fn7hz]. Only I27 says it of herself ({t_linkrolewhose.c22}): "I mean at times it could get quite depressing" [c_links.seu76]; she also says "I don’t find loneliness a massive problem" (I27).

Withdrawing that deepens existing loneliness is said only of others ({t_linkrolewhose.c18}): "you will feel even more lonely because you're, you're like intentionally excluding yourself from things that could actually make you feel even, better" [c_links.ququq]; "for as long as you isolate yourself and you keep yourself away from people, that would only make the situation worse" [c_links.gq65y]. The own accounts of withdrawing leading to loneliness ({t_linkrolewhose.c19}: I27, I41) are about how it starts: "so you remove yourself then you feel lonely" [c_links.hr59e]. Against the idea that withdrawing makes things worse, I08 says of secluding himself "I feel like that's a good way of dealing with it, just do it by yourself" (I08), I41 says "it’s better to feel lonely than sad" (I41), and I39 says "sometimes people are genuinely happy in that state" (I39).

Low mood leading back to loneliness is stated by {t_map.c10}: as a maintaining loop by I41 of others [c_links.ec49r] and by I43 of himself [c_links.n9x4c], and by I39 of herself as a start ("I feel like being upset would sometimes leave you feeling a bit lonely." [c_links.kdsgv]). Loneliness causing withdrawal directly is told by I43 only, of others ({t_linkrolewhose.c24}) [c_links.yvjgr], and loneliness making it hard to open up by I08 only, of others ({t_linkrolewhose.c23}) [c_links.p97kj].

## Chains told by one person

The paths in the coding join each interviewee's own links only, never one person's links to another's. Some are told as one chain about the speaker's own life: I27's move away from friends and family to live near a controlling partner, finding travel to see people too much, and ending up "just me and my son" and isolated [c_links.f8h7g, c_links.335h8, c_links.puzbh], which she then says "could get quite depressing" [c_links.seu76]; I10's failing to keep up family contact for lack of money, then guilt and anxiety that make reconnecting harder [c_links.fns2k, c_links.kawyr, c_links.tsufw], though she does not say this makes her lonely; and I41's anxiety among strangers that makes him leave, "which then makes you feel lonely" [c_links.7tmpj]. Others are links told at different points in an interview and joined by the coding: I27's dread of going out [c_links.xwdun] and her feeling of not wanting to be there when with people [c_links.gkfyt] join low self-worth to loneliness through withdrawing, but she did not tell them as one story. The joined path from withdrawing through loneliness to low mood appears in the links of I08, I27, I41 and I43, but only in I27's are all its steps about the speaker herself. ## Limits

These are the eight interviews the evaluator named, so a count means that many of these people and says nothing about how common a route is among young people. The interviews asked people to draw and talk about the experience of loneliness, so much of what they say is about loneliness in general, and some say they rarely feel it themselves: "For me, I don't really experience a lot of loneliness" (I08); "I don’t think I can relate to this" (I39). The map's numbers count people stating a link of anyone; the table gives own and others separately. Each count is of people who state a link, not of how strong its effect is. What reduces loneliness was not coded, so the map shows causes and loops, not remedies.
