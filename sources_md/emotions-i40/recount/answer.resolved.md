# Feelings in interview I40

The interviewee in I40 gives slightly more unpleasant feelings than pleasant ones: 5 passages negative (and 1 more only weakly), 4 passages positive (and 1 more only weakly) and 1 passage mixed. The unpleasant ones are about being among strangers or alone somewhere unfamiliar, and about crowding; the pleasant ones are about his local hang-out, time on his own, living in the area and, weakly, having a friend with him when lost. He ties 4 passages to loneliness, 2 passages to feeling connected (and 1 more only weakly), and 4 passages to neither (and 1 more only weakly).

## Loneliness among strangers

His loneliness is not about having no friends but about being where he knows no one. On the tube he says "I guess I feel lonely that's the most lonely place I can kind of think of" ([I40, “When I'm on the tube…”](#emotions.as796)), and of Liverpool Street, "It just feels lonely ‘cause there’s no one I know there, not at all" ([I40, “It just feels lonely ‘cause…”](#emotions.sm4nk)). There, he says, "I wanna quickly get in and out of that place" (I40), which he puts with the same loneliness: "seems like everyone is alone as well. That’s why I feel lonely I guess over there" (I40).

Being alone somewhere unfamiliar also makes him nervous. Split up from his friends on holiday and taking a cab on his own, "I was alone by myself so made me feel quite nervous" ([I40, “So, the whole ride was…”](#emotions.cs43f)). He sets this against the same situation with a friend, when "I almost didn't care" ([I40, “the exact same situation happened…”](#emotions.3f6c2)); that passage implies ease rather than naming it, and he names it later only in a general second person: "it just makes you feel at ease" (I40).

One of the feelings he ties to being alone is pleasant. He does not mind, and sometimes wants, time by himself in familiar places: "being alone I wouldn’t say is something I don't like…sometime I’d like to… I would like to you know just go somewhere by myself" ([I40, “being alone I wouldn’t say…”](#emotions.k76es)).

## Crowds and the city

Several unpleasant feelings concern crowding, and these passages do not tie them to loneliness: dislike and awkwardness when the train is packed ([I40, “Pretty crowded. Yeah I don't…”](#emotions.bhncs)), stress that gives way to relief "when the space opens up" ([I40, “It’s just a bit maybe…”](#emotions.7fyn6)), and dislike of the rush at Liverpool Street, "I don't really like it" ([I40, “Yeah when it’s busy, crazy…”](#emotions.gxygs)). He describes tube journeys as "something you just want to get through" ([I40, “I don't know it just…”](#emotions.bhh4s)), in a general second person. Against this he says "I enjoy living here" ([I40, “I mean…I enjoy living here”](#emotions.mc3bs)).

## Feeling connected

Both firm feelings he ties to connection concern the local hang-out by the football pitch, where "Almost every faces are familiar face" (I40): he says he feels "most socially connected" there ([I40, “It’s just really local to…”](#emotions.eyegr)) and "Just very, very comfortable" ([I40, “Just very, very comfortable”](#emotions.2ua92)). The weak one is the ease of being lost on holiday with a friend ([I40, “the exact same situation happened…”](#emotions.3f6c2)).

## Limits

Much of what he says about fear and nervousness is in a general second person about imagined situations, which the definitions leave out; counting those would add negative feelings tied to loneliness.
