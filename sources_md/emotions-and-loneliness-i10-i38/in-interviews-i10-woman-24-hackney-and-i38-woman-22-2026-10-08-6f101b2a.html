Feelings, loneliness and connection in two interviews

Question In interviews I10 (woman, 24, Hackney) and I38 (woman, 22, Tower Hamlets), where does the interviewee express or report their own positive, negative or mixed feelings?

For each example: its valence, a short label for the emotion, what it is about, and whether the interviewee ties it in that passage to loneliness, to feeling connected, or to neither; and how many examples of each valence, and of each link, come from each person. Base: the two people, set side by side, with no wider comparison. Feelings the interviewer suggests and the interviewee does not take up, feelings attributed to other people, and purely hypothetical feelings do not count.

Both women report many feelings of their own, mostly unpleasant ones: of I10's, are negative, positive and mixed; of I38's, are negative, positive and mixed. They differ most in what they tie their feelings to. I38 ties to loneliness, most of them moments of not fitting in: at a new job, at university, among friends who laugh at her or who live differently. I10 ties to loneliness, about shallow friendships, working alone and a time when no one helped her; most of her feelings () she ties to neither, chiefly fear, hopelessness and anger around an abusive relationship and being refused help, and guilt about family she has lost touch with. I38 ties to neither. Each ties a few feelings to connection: I10 , I38 . The contrast in loneliness is clear between these two accounts and would hold if several borderline passages were read the other way; it is a contrast between two people and says nothing about anyone else.

I10: fear, anger and guilt; loneliness about friendships and working alone

I10 ties loneliness to three things: shallow friendships, working alone and an abusive relationship. She says passing party friendships "can allow you to feel lonely" , and that focusing on too many new people brings stress, "upset and distress", and is "where the loneliness comes from" , though she puts this in general terms rather than of herself. Working alone as a freelancer gives the most passages. She loves working for herself but misses "interacting with other people that have a similar skill set as me" ; lacking chances to build her skills "makes me feel like alone from a work sense" ; and after six months on her own she "started to feel really lonely, working alone in the studio" . Two of these passages are positive: she is "much less anxious than I was when I work within a team" , and asked how the loneliness in the studio makes her feel, she answers "Fine, it's not like, it's not awful" . And when she was in an abusive relationship and kept being refused therapy, she "felt really lonely then" .

That episode carries the feelings she holds most strongly, none of which she ties to loneliness in the same passage: she felt "completely completely unsafe" with "three panic attacks a day" , shock and hopelessness at being turned away again and again , and she is still angry: "it just makes me so mad" . She says she is now "pretty content with it" , and, in the same answer as the anger, that she is content with her drug and alcohol use .

Distance from family brings guilt and anxiety about her father's side, "I just don’t even know how to pick up the phone" , guilt at not supporting her mother , and sadness that "I missed out" . Friendships that faded leave a hurt: it "kind of hurts that some people aren’t still in your life" . Her feelings about other people's conduct she also ties to neither: she finds it horrible that people care mainly about themselves , selfishness "makes me feel sad" , the race for followers makes her angry rather than sad , she feels sorry for those who promote themselves that way , she is grateful not to be affected by pressure on body image , and her best friend's leaving social media over it "breaks my heart" .

Her sense of connection rests on a "really really really stable group of friends" whom she loves and trusts , on the excitement of meeting new people , on being approached by other creatives, "that’s really nice" , and on local bars where she feels "most socially connected" . Her other positive feelings are feeling "stronger being able to kind of reject" passing friendships , being glad she invested in her career , and loving a local bar's green approach .

I38: loneliness as not fitting in, connection as acceptance

Most of I38's loneliness comes from feeling she does not fit. Among family and friends who talk about football, "I can’t socially connect with them" . Comparing her two hundred followers with others' thousands makes her wonder what she is doing wrong, and she ends "you still feel lonely" . Starting her first graduate job she "felt a bit lonely" and felt "like an outsider" ; at first she felt "a bit distressed and a bit of anxiety" and "genuinely felt very lonely" ; she envied friends who had made friends at work when "I don’t even have anyone to have lunch with" ; and she "used to go home really upset and lonely" and still feels "a bit frustrated" . A slow job search brought "a sense of loneliness" with feeling "I was incompetent" . At university, as a Muslim among people who drank and went clubbing, she "felt really lonely" and "a bit of an outcast", though that eased and "I don’t mind, it’s okay" . When friends laugh at her anxiety she feels "like an odd black sheep in the group" ; she struggles to open up about personal issues because "they would judge me" ; and she felt friends "still look at me weirdly" for leaving to pray , though in the same answer she says they have since come to respect her religion.

Three of her loneliness passages are not about fitting in: seeing others out when she has "no one to go out with" , enjoying her own company although "it gets a bit boring" , and no longer minding eating lunch on her own .

The feelings she ties to neither are mostly about work and comparison: missing her university freedom , feeling "so robotic when I went to work" , "it’s just that constant fear" of getting things wrong , being "so fed up with this nonsense" at having to accept it , upset at earning less than her friends , friends' posts about lunches at work, which "made me feel really bad", though she is glad she later spoke to them , a sense that others' luxury lives are unfair , dislike of drunken clubbing , and anxiety about her morning routine . Influencers' wealth affected her when she was younger, but now "I’m content and I feel happier" .

Connection, for her, is acceptance without judgement: she feels "socially accepted in these places" at the gym and pool , "a sense of belongingness" at the mosque in Ramadan , "I feel socially connected" at meals with friends and family , and understood by someone who listened rather than laughed .

The two side by side

For I38 loneliness is mainly about not fitting in with the people around her, and connection, found where she is not judged, is its remedy. § I38: loneliness as not fitting in, connection as acceptance I10 ties fewer feelings to loneliness, mostly about shallow friendships and working alone, and two of those are positive feelings about working alone; her strongest feelings concern an abusive relationship and the services that refused her, and many others concern family and other people's conduct. § I10: fear, anger and guilt; loneliness about friendships and working alone Her connection rests chiefly on a long-standing group of close friends. I10 also reports more positive feelings than I38 ( against ); I am fairly sure of this difference too, though less so than of the difference in loneliness, since it rests on how passages are divided.

How far this can be relied on

The counts depend on where one passage is judged to end and the next to begin, since a feeling repeated about the same thing is counted once; they show where each woman's feelings lie, not exact totals. A link counts only where the speaker makes it in the passage, and a feeling about being alone counts as tied to loneliness whatever its valence, which is why two of I10's positive feelings about working alone are among her loneliness passages. Four passages are weak: I10's stress at too many new people and I38's loneliness over followers are put in general terms, and I38's difficulty opening up and going home upset largely restate other passages. Feelings attributed to others are not counted: I10 says people who chase followers "must feel lonely" (I10), but that is about them, not her.

How this was made

The analysis was written down as a workflow before it was counted: what to look for, how to code it, what to count. Code then counted it from the coded passages, so anyone can check a number, and another coder can follow the same definitions and code the interviews afresh.

What was read

All 48 documents, read in full: 24 male, 24 female.

Coding: Emotions

one row for each passage that fits; 60 rows.

The instruction a second coder would follow
Read the interview. Find every passage in which the interviewee (not the interviewer) expresses or reports a feeling of their own: a feeling they have now or have had, in their own words. Loneliness counts as a feeling. A generalised 'you' or 'it can feel' counts only where the interviewee ties it in that passage to their own experience. Do not code: feelings the interviewee imagines for a figure in a drawing or for people in general; purely hypothetical feelings ('I would feel'); feelings the interviewer puts forward that the interviewee only agrees to with 'yeah' or 'uhum'. Where the same feeling about the same thing is repeated or elaborated in follow-up answers, code it once, quoting the clearest words. Where one answer names feelings about two different things, code each. Where one answer names several feelings about the same thing, code them as one passage and name them all in the emotion label; where a later answer names a different feeling about the same thing, code that too. Code a feeling the interviewee has about another person's situation (for example, sadness at a friend's distress) as their own feeling, but not a feeling they say the other person has. A reported absence of feeling ('it doesn't make me feel anything') is not coded. For each passage, copy the words exactly and fill the columns.

Valence

Whether the feeling expressed is pleasant, unpleasant or both.

CodeMeansCountsDoes not count
PositiveA pleasant feeling: happiness, enjoyment, relief, gratitude, love, hope, ease, respect, calm acceptance.Statements such as 'I feel good, happy', 'I enjoy', 'it's alright now'.Neutral description of an activity with no feeling named or plainly expressed.
NegativeAn unpleasant feeling: sadness, loneliness, frustration, resentment, boredom, unease, longing, resignation, distress.Statements such as 'it makes me sad', 'I feel quite lonely', 'I don't feel okay'.A passage that pairs an unpleasant feeling with a pleasant or accepting one about the same thing: code mixed.
MixedPleasant and unpleasant feelings, or acceptance and hurt, expressed together about the same thing.Statements such as 'conflicting emotions' or 'I'm used to it now but sometimes it can get to me'. Also an unpleasant feeling in the past and a pleasant or accepting one now about the same thing, in the same passage ('when I was a lot younger, it did affect me ... I’m content and I feel happier').Two feelings about different things in the same answer: code each separately.

Emotion

A short label, one to three words, for the feeling, such as loneliness, frustration, gratitude, relief; where one passage names several feelings, list each.

Subject

In a few words, what the feeling is about or what brings it on, as the interviewee describes it.

Link

Whether the interviewee, in the coded passage itself, ties the feeling to loneliness or to feeling connected.

CodeMeansCountsDoes not count
LonelinessThe feeling is loneliness, or the interviewee ties it in the passage to loneliness, being alone, having no one, not fitting in, or being an outsider or 'black sheep'.'that sometimes makes me feel lonely'; 'why do I feel like an outsider'; 'I don't mind if I don't sit with anyone' (a feeling about being alone, whatever its valence). A feeling about being or working alone counts whatever its valence, including relief or enjoyment at working alone rather than with others ('much less anxious than I was when I work within a team').A feeling about a situation the interviewee elsewhere calls lonely, where this passage does not make the tie; loneliness the interviewee attributes to other people. Enjoyment of freedom or independence where the passage does not speak of being alone or without other people (code neither).
ConnectionThe interviewee ties the feeling in the passage to feeling connected, belonging, being accepted or understood, or closeness and enjoyment with other people.'a sense of belongingness'; 'I felt like they understood me'; 'I love them and trust them with everything'.Enjoyment of a place, activity or freedom with no other people named as the source of the feeling.
NeitherThe passage ties the feeling to neither loneliness nor connection.Anger at a service, guilt about family contact, anxiety about a routine, where the passage names no tie.Any passage that fits loneliness or connection.
