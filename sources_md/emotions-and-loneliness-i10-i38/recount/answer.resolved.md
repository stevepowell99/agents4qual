# Feelings, loneliness and connection in two interviews

Both women report many feelings of their own, mostly unpleasant ones: of I10's, 17 passages are negative, 12 passages positive and 1 passages mixed; of I38's, 21 passages are negative, 5 passages positive and 4 passages mixed. They differ most in what they tie their feelings to. I38 ties 16 passages to loneliness, most of them moments of not fitting in: at a new job, at university, among friends who laugh at her or who live differently. I10 ties 8 passages to loneliness, about shallow friendships, working alone and a time when no one helped her; most of her feelings (18 passages) she ties to neither, chiefly fear, hopelessness and anger around an abusive relationship and being refused help, and guilt about family she has lost touch with. I38 ties 10 passages to neither. Each ties a few feelings to connection: I10 4 passages, I38 4 passages. The contrast in loneliness is clear between these two accounts and would hold if several borderline passages were read the other way; it is a contrast between two people and says nothing about anyone else.

## I10: fear, anger and guilt; loneliness about friendships and working alone

I10 ties loneliness to three things: shallow friendships, working alone and an abusive relationship. She says passing party friendships "can allow you to feel lonely" [emotions.5yvpx], and that focusing on too many new people brings stress, "upset and distress", and is "where the loneliness comes from" [emotions.bvdj2], though she puts this in general terms rather than of herself. Working alone as a freelancer gives the most passages. She loves working for herself but misses "interacting with other people that have a similar skill set as me" [emotions.pe48r]; lacking chances to build her skills "makes me feel like alone from a work sense" [emotions.hy9s6]; and after six months on her own she "started to feel really lonely, working alone in the studio" [emotions.gwxc6]. Two of these passages are positive: she is "much less anxious than I was when I work within a team" [emotions.hm3mb], and asked how the loneliness in the studio makes her feel, she answers "Fine, it's not like, it's not awful" [emotions.24qqp]. And when she was in an abusive relationship and kept being refused therapy, she "felt really lonely then" [emotions.nepvf].

That episode carries the feelings she holds most strongly, none of which she ties to loneliness in the same passage: she felt "completely completely unsafe" with "three panic attacks a day" [emotions.txk67], shock and hopelessness at being turned away again and again [emotions.k3wjn], and she is still angry: "it just makes me so mad" [emotions.vmp6r]. She says she is now "pretty content with it" [emotions.zcffv], and, in the same answer as the anger, that she is content with her drug and alcohol use [emotions.6msjp].

Distance from family brings guilt and anxiety about her father's side, "I just don’t even know how to pick up the phone" [emotions.v2h4y], guilt at not supporting her mother [emotions.xf2qm], and sadness that "I missed out" [emotions.w6nvx]. Friendships that faded leave a hurt: it "kind of hurts that some people aren’t still in your life" [emotions.txw2y]. Her feelings about other people's conduct she also ties to neither: she finds it horrible that people care mainly about themselves [emotions.kjhzf], selfishness "makes me feel sad" [emotions.dswxv], the race for followers makes her angry rather than sad [emotions.fzsu3], she feels sorry for those who promote themselves that way [emotions.nyaqg], she is grateful not to be affected by pressure on body image [emotions.nvumz], and her best friend's leaving social media over it "breaks my heart" [emotions.aqfpp].

Her sense of connection rests on a "really really really stable group of friends" whom she loves and trusts [emotions.n256w], on the excitement of meeting new people [emotions.fvy4u], on being approached by other creatives, "that’s really nice" [emotions.uepqn], and on local bars where she feels "most socially connected" [emotions.dsxt9]. Her other positive feelings are feeling "stronger being able to kind of reject" passing friendships [emotions.fnvsa], being glad she invested in her career [emotions.2xpsk], and loving a local bar's green approach [emotions.p8rwz].

## I38: loneliness as not fitting in, connection as acceptance

Most of I38's loneliness comes from feeling she does not fit. Among family and friends who talk about football, "I can’t socially connect with them" [emotions.2hts6]. Comparing her two hundred followers with others' thousands makes her wonder what she is doing wrong, and she ends "you still feel lonely" [emotions.t7bz6]. Starting her first graduate job she "felt a bit lonely" [emotions.ugqbf] and felt "like an outsider" [emotions.cv98s]; at first she felt "a bit distressed and a bit of anxiety" and "genuinely felt very lonely" [emotions.dk77r]; she envied friends who had made friends at work when "I don’t even have anyone to have lunch with" [emotions.ce2qf]; and she "used to go home really upset and lonely" and still feels "a bit frustrated" [emotions.w687b]. A slow job search brought "a sense of loneliness" with feeling "I was incompetent" [emotions.c552z]. At university, as a Muslim among people who drank and went clubbing, she "felt really lonely" [emotions.n3gjx] and "a bit of an outcast", though that eased and "I don’t mind, it’s okay" [emotions.695xj]. When friends laugh at her anxiety she feels "like an odd black sheep in the group" [emotions.pejju]; she struggles to open up about personal issues because "they would judge me" [emotions.f8b6f]; and she felt friends "still look at me weirdly" for leaving to pray [emotions.2e7h2], though in the same answer she says they have since come to respect her religion.

Three of her loneliness passages are not about fitting in: seeing others out when she has "no one to go out with" [emotions.6za9a], enjoying her own company although "it gets a bit boring" [emotions.tevgp], and no longer minding eating lunch on her own [emotions.8btd8].

The feelings she ties to neither are mostly about work and comparison: missing her university freedom [emotions.nf2vh], feeling "so robotic when I went to work" [emotions.kn327], "it’s just that constant fear" of getting things wrong [emotions.y6w7d], being "so fed up with this nonsense" at having to accept it [emotions.hhzxv], upset at earning less than her friends [emotions.7s485], friends' posts about lunches at work, which "made me feel really bad", though she is glad she later spoke to them [emotions.53k7h], a sense that others' luxury lives are unfair [emotions.unjpb], dislike of drunken clubbing [emotions.rcrcg], and anxiety about her morning routine [emotions.674m2]. Influencers' wealth affected her when she was younger, but now "I’m content and I feel happier" [emotions.32pq7].

Connection, for her, is acceptance without judgement: she feels "socially accepted in these places" at the gym and pool [emotions.w6nyw], "a sense of belongingness" at the mosque in Ramadan [emotions.zpraa], "I feel socially connected" at meals with friends and family [emotions.k4vtm], and understood by someone who listened rather than laughed [emotions.t689v].

## The two side by side

For I38 loneliness is mainly about not fitting in with the people around her, and connection, found where she is not judged, is its remedy. [§ I38: loneliness as not fitting in, connection as acceptance] I10 ties fewer feelings to loneliness, mostly about shallow friendships and working alone, and two of those are positive feelings about working alone; her strongest feelings concern an abusive relationship and the services that refused her, and many others concern family and other people's conduct. [§ I10: fear, anger and guilt; loneliness about friendships and working alone] Her connection rests chiefly on a long-standing group of close friends. I10 also reports more positive feelings than I38 (12 passages against 5 passages); I am fairly sure of this difference too, though less so than of the difference in loneliness, since it rests on how passages are divided.

## How far this can be relied on

The counts depend on where one passage is judged to end and the next to begin, since a feeling repeated about the same thing is counted once; they show where each woman's feelings lie, not exact totals. A link counts only where the speaker makes it in the passage, and a feeling about being alone counts as tied to loneliness whatever its valence, which is why two of I10's positive feelings about working alone are among her loneliness passages. Four passages are weak: I10's stress at too many new people [emotions.bvdj2] and I38's loneliness over followers [emotions.t7bz6] are put in general terms, and I38's difficulty opening up [emotions.f8b6f] and going home upset [emotions.w687b] largely restate other passages. Feelings attributed to others are not counted: I10 says people who chase followers "must feel lonely" (I10), but that is about them, not her.
