# The feelings expressed in two loneliness interviews

The feelings the person from Newham (I01) expresses are negative in {by_person.c3}, positive in {by_person.c4} and mixed in {by_person.c5}; those of the woman from Barking & Dagenham (I48) are negative in {by_person.c1}, positive in {by_person.c2} and mixed in {by_person.c6}. Both express more negative than positive feelings, and loneliness recurs among the negative ones of both. Each example is one feeling, or several named together in one answer, about one thing, counted once however often it is repeated. They place their loneliness in not being understood, after breakups and in the shopping centre where people sleep rough; she places hers at home, at night and in the loss of her mother. Their good feelings come from the barber shop's familiar community, from time alone that they choose and from respect for people who help rough sleepers; hers come mostly from being with people, which keeps her thoughts at bay, and also from her love of children, her hope of a child of her own and her acceptance of her mother's death.

## The person from Newham (I01)

Their negative feelings centre on being misunderstood. They say "I’ve always had people around me but I’ve been more misunderstood, which has led to being lonely" [emotions.v87a4], and calls it "quite frustrating" [emotions.gw77h]. They also recall times "where I felt unsupported" [emotions.rcepv] and says that after their breakups "you feel more lonelier after than you was before" [emotions.6ygx6].

Walking through the shopping centre after 7pm past people sleeping rough brings out three feelings: loneliness ("I think I feel quite lonely in that moment" [emotions.z82hd]), resentment towards those responsible ("I feel completely resentment when I walk through those environments and see those people sleeping rough" [emotions.a2qbn]), and doubt about whether to give money, which they call "conflicting emotions" [emotions.38far].

Their positive feelings are about the barber shop ("it feels good to go, um I mean I enjoy the barber shop", and "I’m grateful to see people that I know and have known for a long time fine" [emotions.rcr2x]), about time alone that they choose ("I’m quite at ease with that situation" [emotions.gqkh7]), and about people who run soup kitchens, of whom they say "I respect and appreciate" [emotions.7nrgg].

## The woman from Barking & Dagenham (I48)

Her negative feelings return again and again to being alone. Seeing friends with their mothers "really makes me sad, it makes me feel lonely" [emotions.b4tzb], and she says "I wished I had my mom" [emotions.asuzr]; asked how she feels about not being able to sleep, she says "I feel like I can’t change that. I can’t bring my mom back." [emotions.z9fyf]. Seeing friends with siblings is "annoying, sad, lonely" and "makes me wish I can do what they do with their siblings" [emotions.wpafh]. Alone, she reports that "It’s not a nice feeling, knowing that there’s no one" [emotions.jd2fs], that "sometimes I just remember things and I’m not happy about that" [emotions.87mj7], "I don’t feel okay" [emotions.2mh46] and "I feel like I need help sometimes" [emotions.w5b27]. At night she sometimes has "to cry myself to sleep because I’m lonely" [emotions.dq9j6]; at home, when the house is quiet, she realises "I’m lonely, starts all over again" [emotions.g7jc9] and feels bored [emotions.qdeac]. About having no one to talk to about her day, she says "I’m used to it now but sometimes it can get to me" [emotions.q2udu].

Her positive feelings come mostly from company and activity: "worry-free around my friends" [emotions.vctax], "I feel good, happy" when around people [emotions.va4p8], fun when clubbing that "makes me forget about, you know, life" [emotions.cy9ju], and "Good, relieved" about work and her colleagues there [emotions.fyr8k], where she also says "I love kids so much" [emotions.una4g]. She also expresses hope of having her own child one day [emotions.qsvzx], and says of her mother's death "It’s alright now. It’s cool." [emotions.4bc53], an acceptance that sits beside her later longing for her mother [emotions.asuzr].

They speak about loneliness at some distance, often in the second person and in reflective terms; she names her feelings briefly and directly, and several of her negative feelings are answers to the interviewer's repeated questions about how something makes her feel. Most of the positive feelings of both come from being with familiar people, and for her they work mainly as relief from what she feels alone [§ The woman from Barking & Dagenham (I48)]; against this, they also feel at ease in time alone that they choose [emotions.gqkh7].

## Limits

These are two interviews, so they describe two people and nothing wider. The counts depend on where one feeling ends and the next begins, and on excluding hypothetical feelings and feelings they imagine for figures in their drawings, which are a large part of their first interview; a different reader could split or merge some examples. Both interviews asked directly about loneliness, which draws out negative feelings.
