# The feelings expressed in two loneliness interviews

The feelings the person from Newham (I01) expresses are negative in 6 passages, positive in 3 passages and mixed in 1 passage; those of the woman from Barking & Dagenham (I48) are negative in 11 passages, positive in 7 passages and mixed in 1 passage. Both express more negative than positive feelings, and loneliness recurs among the negative ones of both. Each example is one feeling, or several named together in one answer, about one thing, counted once however often it is repeated. They place their loneliness in not being understood, after breakups and in the shopping centre where people sleep rough; she places hers at home, at night and in the loss of her mother. Their good feelings come from the barber shop's familiar community, from time alone that they choose and from respect for people who help rough sleepers; hers come mostly from being with people, which keeps her thoughts at bay, and also from her love of children, her hope of a child of her own and her acceptance of her mother's death.

## The person from Newham (I01)

Their negative feelings centre on being misunderstood. They say "I’ve always had people around me but I’ve been more misunderstood, which has led to being lonely" ([I01, “I’ve always had people around…”](#emotions.v87a4)), and calls it "quite frustrating" ([I01, “Uuum, quite frustrating. It’s frustrating…”](#emotions.gw77h)). They also recall times "where I felt unsupported" ([I01, “probably, a couple of times…”](#emotions.rcepv)) and says that after their breakups "you feel more lonelier after than you was before" ([I01, “I was always just as…”](#emotions.6ygx6)).

Walking through the shopping centre after 7pm past people sleeping rough brings out three feelings: loneliness ("I think I feel quite lonely in that moment" ([I01, “I think I feel quite…”](#emotions.z82hd))), resentment towards those responsible ("I feel completely resentment when I walk through those environments and see those people sleeping rough" ([I01, “I feel completely resentment when…”](#emotions.a2qbn))), and doubt about whether to give money, which they call "conflicting emotions" ([I01, “I wouldn’t want to contribute…”](#emotions.38far)).

Their positive feelings are about the barber shop ("it feels good to go, um I mean I enjoy the barber shop", and "I’m grateful to see people that I know and have known for a long time fine" ([I01, “it feels good to go…”](#emotions.rcr2x))), about time alone that they choose ("I’m quite at ease with that situation" ([I01, “when I choose to kind…”](#emotions.gqkh7))), and about people who run soup kitchens, of whom they say "I respect and appreciate" ([I01, “I’ve seen how some people…”](#emotions.7nrgg)).

## The woman from Barking & Dagenham (I48)

Her negative feelings return again and again to being alone. Seeing friends with their mothers "really makes me sad, it makes me feel lonely" ([I48, “it really makes me sad…”](#emotions.b4tzb)), and she says "I wished I had my mom" ([I48, “Just, I wished I had…”](#emotions.asuzr)); asked how she feels about not being able to sleep, she says "I feel like I can’t change that. I can’t bring my mom back." ([I48, “I feel like I can’t…”](#emotions.z9fyf)). Seeing friends with siblings is "annoying, sad, lonely" and "makes me wish I can do what they do with their siblings" ([I48, “It’s annoying, sad, lonely. [pause]…”](#emotions.wpafh)). Alone, she reports that "It’s not a nice feeling, knowing that there’s no one" ([I48, “It’s not a nice feeling…”](#emotions.jd2fs)), that "sometimes I just remember things and I’m not happy about that" ([I48, “sometimes I just remember things…”](#emotions.87mj7)), "I don’t feel okay" ([I48, “I don’t feel okay. I…”](#emotions.2mh46)) and "I feel like I need help sometimes" ([I48, “I feel like I need…”](#emotions.w5b27)). At night she sometimes has "to cry myself to sleep because I’m lonely" ([I48, “at night before going to…”](#emotions.dq9j6)); at home, when the house is quiet, she realises "I’m lonely, starts all over again" ([I48, “then I realise, oh my…”](#emotions.g7jc9)) and feels bored ([I48, “Just bored cause there’s nothing…”](#emotions.qdeac)). About having no one to talk to about her day, she says "I’m used to it now but sometimes it can get to me" ([I48, “I feel like I’m used…”](#emotions.q2udu)).

Her positive feelings come mostly from company and activity: "worry-free around my friends" ([I48, “It makes worry-free around my…”](#emotions.vctax)), "I feel good, happy" when around people ([I48, “I feel good, happy”](#emotions.va4p8)), fun when clubbing that "makes me forget about, you know, life" ([I48, “I see people having fun…”](#emotions.cy9ju)), and "Good, relieved" about work and her colleagues there ([I48, “Good, relieved”](#emotions.fyr8k)), where she also says "I love kids so much" ([I48, “I love kids so much”](#emotions.una4g)). She also expresses hope of having her own child one day ([I48, “I just hope that one…”](#emotions.qsvzx)), and says of her mother's death "It’s alright now. It’s cool." ([I48, “It’s alright now. It’s cool”](#emotions.4bc53)), an acceptance that sits beside her later longing for her mother ([I48, “Just, I wished I had…”](#emotions.asuzr)).

## Limits

These are two interviews, so they describe two people and nothing wider. The counts depend on where one feeling ends and the next begins, and on excluding hypothetical feelings and feelings they imagine for figures in their drawings, which are a large part of their first interview; a different reader could split or merge some examples. Both interviews asked directly about loneliness, which draws out negative feelings.
