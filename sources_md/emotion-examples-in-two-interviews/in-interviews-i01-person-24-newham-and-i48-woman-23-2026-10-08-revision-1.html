Feelings expressed in two loneliness interviews

Question In interviews I01 (person, 24, Newham) and I48 (woman, 23, Barking & Dagenham), where does the interviewee express or report their own positive, negative or mixed feelings?

For each example: its valence, a short label for the emotion, and what it is about; and how many examples of each valence come from each person. Base: the two people; no comparison beyond setting them side by side. Feelings the interviewer suggests and the interviewee does not take up, and feelings the interviewee attributes to other people, do not count.

The feelings the person from Newham (I01) expresses are negative in , positive in and mixed in ; those of the woman from Barking & Dagenham (I48) are negative in , positive in and mixed in . Both express more negative than positive feelings, and loneliness recurs among the negative ones of both. Each example is one feeling, or several named together in one answer, about one thing, counted once however often it is repeated. They place their loneliness in not being understood, after breakups and in the shopping centre where people sleep rough; she places hers at home, at night and in the loss of her mother. Their good feelings come from the barber shop's familiar community, from time alone that they choose and from respect for people who help rough sleepers; hers come mostly from being with people, which keeps her thoughts at bay, and also from her love of children, her hope of a child of her own and her acceptance of her mother's death.

The person from Newham (I01)

Their negative feelings centre on being misunderstood. They say "I’ve always had people around me but I’ve been more misunderstood, which has led to being lonely" , and calls it "quite frustrating" . They also recall times "where I felt unsupported" and says that after their breakups "you feel more lonelier after than you was before" .

Walking through the shopping centre after 7pm past people sleeping rough brings out three feelings: loneliness ("I think I feel quite lonely in that moment" ), resentment towards those responsible ("I feel completely resentment when I walk through those environments and see those people sleeping rough" ), and doubt about whether to give money, which they call "conflicting emotions" .

Their positive feelings are about the barber shop ("it feels good to go, um I mean I enjoy the barber shop", and "I’m grateful to see people that I know and have known for a long time fine" ), about time alone that they choose ("I’m quite at ease with that situation" ), and about people who run soup kitchens, of whom they say "I respect and appreciate" .

The woman from Barking & Dagenham (I48)

Her negative feelings return again and again to being alone. Seeing friends with their mothers "really makes me sad, it makes me feel lonely" , and she says "I wished I had my mom" ; asked how she feels about not being able to sleep, she says "I feel like I can’t change that. I can’t bring my mom back." . Seeing friends with siblings is "annoying, sad, lonely" and "makes me wish I can do what they do with their siblings" . Alone, she reports that "It’s not a nice feeling, knowing that there’s no one" , that "sometimes I just remember things and I’m not happy about that" , "I don’t feel okay" and "I feel like I need help sometimes" . At night she sometimes has "to cry myself to sleep because I’m lonely" ; at home, when the house is quiet, she realises "I’m lonely, starts all over again" and feels bored . About having no one to talk to about her day, she says "I’m used to it now but sometimes it can get to me" .

Her positive feelings come mostly from company and activity: "worry-free around my friends" , "I feel good, happy" when around people , fun when clubbing that "makes me forget about, you know, life" , and "Good, relieved" about work and her colleagues there , where she also says "I love kids so much" . She also expresses hope of having her own child one day , and says of her mother's death "It’s alright now. It’s cool." , an acceptance that sits beside her later longing for her mother .

Limits

These are two interviews, so they describe two people and nothing wider. The counts depend on where one feeling ends and the next begins, and on excluding hypothetical feelings and feelings they imagine for figures in their drawings, which are a large part of their first interview; a different reader could split or merge some examples. Both interviews asked directly about loneliness, which draws out negative feelings.

How this was made

The analysis was written down as a workflow before it was counted: what to look for, how to code it, what to count. Code then counted it from the coded passages, so anyone can check a number, and another coder can follow the same definitions and code the interviews afresh.

What was read

All 48 documents, read in full: 24 male, 24 female.

Coding: Emotions

one row for each passage that fits; 29 rows.

The instruction a second coder would follow
Read the interview. Find every passage in which the interviewee (not the interviewer) expresses or reports a feeling of their own: a feeling they have now or have had, in their own words. Loneliness counts as a feeling. A generalised 'you' or 'it can feel' counts only where the interviewee ties it in that passage to their own experience. Do not code: feelings the interviewee imagines for a figure in a drawing or for people in general; purely hypothetical feelings ('I would feel'); feelings the interviewer puts forward that the interviewee only agrees to with 'yeah' or 'uhum'. Where the same feeling about the same thing is repeated or elaborated in follow-up answers, code it once, quoting the clearest words. Where one answer names feelings about two different things, code each. Where one answer names several feelings about the same thing, code them as one passage and name them all in the emotion label; where a later answer names a different feeling about the same thing, code that too. For each passage, copy the words exactly and fill the columns.

Valence

Whether the feeling expressed is pleasant, unpleasant or both.

CodeMeansCountsDoes not count
PositiveA pleasant feeling: happiness, enjoyment, relief, gratitude, love, hope, ease, respect, calm acceptance.Statements such as 'I feel good, happy', 'I enjoy', 'it's alright now'.Neutral description of an activity with no feeling named or plainly expressed.
NegativeAn unpleasant feeling: sadness, loneliness, frustration, resentment, boredom, unease, longing, resignation, distress.Statements such as 'it makes me sad', 'I feel quite lonely', 'I don't feel okay'.A passage that pairs an unpleasant feeling with a pleasant or accepting one about the same thing: code mixed.
MixedPleasant and unpleasant feelings, or acceptance and hurt, expressed together about the same thing.Statements such as 'conflicting emotions' or 'I'm used to it now but sometimes it can get to me'.Two feelings about different things in the same answer: code each separately.

Emotion

A short label, one to three words, for the feeling, such as loneliness, frustration, gratitude, relief; where one passage names several feelings, list each.

Subject

In a few words, what the feeling is about or what brings it on, as the interviewee describes it.
