# Procedure

1. Documents. The question names interviews I01 and I48 from `corpus/` (listed in `corpus/index.csv`); the workflow's sample step `two` selects them by id. No other document was read.
2. First coding. The analyst (Claude, Opus) read both transcripts in full and coded every passage in which the interviewee expresses or reports a feeling of their own, to the prompt and columns of the code step `emotions` in `workflow.json` (valence: positive, negative or mixed; emotion label; subject). Rows are in `coded/emotions.json`; passages considered and left out are in `work/borderline.md`; the per-document table of passages is `work/evidence-table.md` (updated after the check).
3. Recount. `recount.py` placed every quotation in its document, checked every value, and counted rows by document and valence (tabulate step `by_person`). Output in `recount/`.
4. Second coding. A fresh helper (Sonnet) recoded both documents to the same definitions without seeing the first coding, under the brief in `work/briefs/recode.md`. Its rows are in `recode/coded/emotions.json`, its notes in `recode/unclear.md`; `recode/disagreements.md` compares the two codings by document and value, and `work/passage_compare.py` (output `work/passage_compare_output.md`) compares them passage by passage.
5. Check. A second fresh helper (Opus) ruled on every difference, tightened the step's prompt where the second coder's notes showed a gap, corrected the rows and the answer, and recounted, under the brief in `work/briefs/check.md`. Its rulings and changes are in `check.md`.
6. Answer. `answer.md` cites every count as a cell id of `by_person` and every claim by row id; `recount.py --answer` resolves the counts and checks every citation and quotation (`recount/answer.resolved.md`).
