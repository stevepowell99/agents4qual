- 20 October 2026: In the 12 pilot interviews I04, I06, I07, I17, I18, I24, I28, I29, I37, I42, I43 and I48 (drawn at random within each borough and gender from the 48 interviews with young Londoners about loneliness), what different roles do the young people describe technology and social media playing in their own positive and negative moods, how many people describe each role, and on which side of mood? The case is one person (one interview); a person can describe several roles, on both sides. A passage counts where the speaker ties a technology to a feeling of their own, positive or negative, including feeling lonely or feeling connected. Technology covers social media, messaging, phone and video calls, gaming, streaming and the internet generally. Any role counts: starting a feeling, keeping it going, easing it or lifting it. Feelings the interviewer suggests and the speaker does not take up, feelings of other people, and general remarks about social media or "people these days" that the speaker does not apply to themselves do not count. Accounts the interviewer prompted by raising technology count, but are marked, so the answer can say how many raised a role unprompted. The roles are drawn from the interviews: each passage is coded openly in the speaker's words, the labels are gathered into a short list of roles (a soft recode of the open coding, each passage read once), and the evaluator sees the list with an example passage for each before anything is counted by it. Every passage of the 12 interviews is read in full. The answer describes the roles, counted by person, with quotations, and gives no verdict. A fresh reader checks the answer against the passages it cites and the documents before it is shown (a check, not a blind recode). Separately from the answer, report anything in the documents that bears on the question but does not fit the frame it sets, each point once, in its own section, which the rest of the answer points to rather than repeats.
